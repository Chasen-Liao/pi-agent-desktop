# Formats — what kind of film is this

A format fixes length, frame, structure and ending. The language (how things move) is chosen
separately; the table at the end of each format lists the languages that usually fit.

Times are starting points for a skeleton, measured from top films (Sep–Oct 2026); retime to the
content and the music. `B` = beats at the film's tempo.

---

## Launch sting · 6–15 s

The thing people repost when a product ships. One idea, one hero moment, the mark.

- Frame: 16:9 or 1:1 for X; 9:16 cut for Reels. 60 fps for UI-heavy stings.
- Skeleton (120 BPM, 32 B ≈ 16 s): hook in motion (0–4 B) → the product doing the one thing (4–16 B)
  → hero moment on the drop (16 B) → payoff/benefit line (20–26 B) → mark + name (26–32 B), last
  frame = first frame if it will loop.
- Sound: one track with a drop; 8–15 hits; mark gets a chime or sparkle.
- Languages: continuous-morph, cursor-demo, kinetic-type, 3d-object, showreel.
- Checks: understandable muted; the product name is on screen ≥ 1.5 s; one key phrase, not five.
- Brand ident variant (7 of 48 Oct 2026 launch films, 6 with no hard cut): no UI at all; one object,
  line drawing or wireframe resolves into the mark (3d-object, system-diagram, continuous-morph line
  drawing, generative-field for a model teaser). Often silent; the ending can return to the dot or
  shape that opened it.

## Launch film · 30–90 s

The announcement video. Problem → product → proof → mark. Often with narration or a founder on camera.

- Skeleton: cold open on the result or the problem (0–5 s) → the product in use, 2–4 moments that
  each show a different capability (5–60 s) → often a non-UI spectacle right after the demos (a
  chain reaction, a particle gather, a duel) → proof (a number, a customer, a stack of logos) →
  availability line + mark, held 2–4 s, URL pill optional. Ending on a music cut-out with one line works.
- Nearly half of the Oct 2026 launch films told a meta-story: the film itself was made by the product
  (a prompt is typed and the film is the result; a pull-back reveals the editor).
- Narration or a person on camera mainly in films over 60 s; most shorter launches had neither.
- Climax at 70–85 % of the length, not at the end; the last seconds resolve.
- Languages: cursor-demo as the spine; a second language for the claim moments (kinetic-type,
  data-story); talking-head when a person narrates.
- Checks: each capability gets a distinct visual moment; no feature list read over one static screen.

## Feature demo · 10–45 s

"Here's the new thing and how you use it." The most common official post.

- Skeleton: the context (where in the product, 1–2 s) → the action (cursor, typing) → the response
  (the product does it) → the result, held long enough to read → optional second variation → end.
- Follow [product-demo-continuity.md](../product-demo-continuity.md) for framing.
- Languages: cursor-demo, continuous-morph (for small UI features), liquid-glass (consumer iOS).
- Checks: action → response → result is visible for every step; nothing is "click and it's done"
  unless it really is.

## Explainer · 30–180 s

Teach how or why something works. Usually narrated.

- Skeleton: question/hook (0–5 s) → intuition (one picture) → mechanism in steps, one transformation
  per sentence → the payoff / so-what → recap image.
- Languages: mechanism (3b1b), system-diagram, flat-world, hand-drawn, editorial-collage.
- Checks: every sentence has a visible change; the final frame shows the whole system.

## Data story · 15–60 s

A number, a trend, a comparison. Earnings, growth, research findings, a milestone.

- Skeleton: the question → the baseline → the change (draw / count / morph) → the turning point
  highlighted → the takeaway sentence over the chart → source line.
- Languages: data-story, kinetic-type (single number), modular-grid.
- Checks: bars start at zero; numbers carry their source; the takeaway is computable from the chart.

## Kinetic manifesto · 8–30 s

Words are the film. A slogan, a list of beliefs, a hype cut for an event or a launch.

- Skeleton: one word or phrase per beat or two; a build of 3–4 statements; a break (silence or
  a single word); the payoff line; mark.
- Languages: kinetic-type, modular-grid, showreel.
- Checks: every word is readable at 1× speed; the payoff has the most space.

## Logo reveal / end card · 3–6 s

The brand mark assembling. Also the ending of every other format.

- Skeleton: elements gather (or one shape morphs) → the mark resolves on a hit → the wordmark
  appears (mask reveal, wipe from behind the mark) → hold ≥ 1 s.
- Languages: continuous-morph, kinetic-type (accordion squeeze into the period), 3d-object, generative-field.
- Checks: the mark is drawn from the real logo file, not approximated.

## Showreel · 10–20 s

"Look what this can do." A portfolio, an agency reel, a model's capabilities, "made with X".

- Skeleton: about 8 sections of 1–1.3 s (two beats each at 120–130 BPM) in 15 s, each a different
  technique or style, on a single spine (one colour system, HUD chrome, a recurring shape, one
  track) so it reads as one piece. Reels of 30 s and longer did markedly worse in Sep–Oct 2026.
- Languages: showreel (style-hopping on a spine), art-styles, generative-field.
- Checks: transitions carry a shape between sections; the spine is visible in every section;
  small mono readouts are either readable or plainly texture.

## Social hook / ad · 6–20 s, 9:16

Performance ads, UGC-style promos, TikTok/Reels/小红书.

- Skeleton: the hook in the first 0.5 s (a question on screen, a striking image, a result first) →
  the problem/desire → product moment → offer/CTA with the name on screen ≥ 2 s.
- Burned-in captions if there is speech; safe zones (top 220 px, bottom 520 px at 1080×1920).
- Languages: kinetic-type, cursor-demo (phone), photo-led, character.

## Resource list loop · 8–25 s

A named list that loops: "20 skills for…", "8 tools I use". Numbered items, a highlight scanning
across them, a counter, often a mascot. The most-saved format of the Claude Motion wave (saves 1.3–2.4×
likes): it gives people something to keep.

- Skeleton: title with the count → items one by one with a chapter counter (≈2–2.5 s each) → the full
  wall → back to the first frame.
- Languages: kinetic-type, modular-grid, retro-digital (terminal HUD), editorial-collage (overview
  wall → zoom into each → back to the wall with labels).
- Checks: every item readable when it is highlighted; the names are real.

## Prompt reel / gallery · 12–35 s

Motion pieces with the prompt or code that made them shown beside them; cards play as they enter.
For tools and skills whose output is the product.

- Languages: showreel, modular-grid. Checks: prompts are the real ones; output and prompt match.

## Game trailer · 30–60 s

A low-poly world, a follow camera, chapter markers, "PLAY FREE" and a URL.

- Languages: 3d-object, kinetic-type for chapter cards, character.

## Loop · 4–12 s

Landing-page hero, README GIF, App Store preview loop, ambient background.

- Skeleton: a cycle that returns to its start — the last frame equals the first, including velocities.
- No sound (or optional), no text that needs reading on first pass, subtle enough to sit behind a
  headline (for heroes) or self-explanatory (for README GIFs).
- Languages: continuous-morph, generative-field, modular-grid, 3d-object.
- Checks: `om qa --loop`; file size for GIF/WebM targets.

## Talking-head package · any length

Name plates, number cards, keyword 花字, chapter cards over a person talking. See
[talking-head.md](../talking-head.md); starts from the `talking-head` template.

## Step promo · 15–30 s

A small product's film as 3–6 numbered steps: one line and one working card per screen, a
progress system across the cuts, a completion moment, the URL. The most common shape for a small
team's product film in Sep–Oct 2026 (card-montage).

- Skeleton: problem line (2–3 s) → mark → step screens (2–3 s each) → completion receipt → logo,
  promise, URL.
- Languages: card-montage; cursor-demo inside a step; kinetic-type for the hook.
- Checks: every card does something while on screen; the steps can be retold after one view.

## Tutorial / walkthrough · 30 s–5 min

Step-by-step, followable, usually narrated.

- Skeleton: the goal shown first (what you'll have at the end) → numbered steps, each with action →
  result → recap.
- Languages: cursor-demo, hand-drawn annotation over UI.
- Checks: a viewer can pause at each step and reproduce it.

## Recap / milestone · 15–45 s

Year in review, "1 million visitors", a changelog of the month.

- Skeleton: the headline number first or last; a rhythm of items (one per beat); a moment of thanks.
- Languages: data-story, kinetic-type, modular-grid, editorial-collage (screenshots of moments).

## Before / after · 6–20 s

(Also: the original and the recreation running side by side on two timelines.)

A transformation: old vs new, without vs with, competitor vs us (only factual).

- Skeleton: before state (long enough to understand the pain) → the transformation device (wipe,
  morph, flood) → after state held → the label of what changed.
- Languages: continuous-morph, data-story, cursor-demo.
- Checks: before and after are framed identically so the difference is the only change.
