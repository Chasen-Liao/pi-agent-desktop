# showreel — many techniques on one spine

A designer's (or a model's) reel: 5–8 short sections, each a different technique or style, held
together by a spine — one colour system, HUD chrome (corner brackets, timecode, BPM, `02 — EASING`),
a recurring shape, one track. The most-liked Opus motion videos of Sep 2026 were showreels ("show
what an incredible motion designer you are… go all out").

**Says:** *range and mastery.* Also: *this tool/model can do all of this.*

**Use when:** capability showcases, agency/portfolio pieces, "made with X" launches, model releases.
**Avoid when:** a product needs one clear message (a reel is about the maker, not the user).

## Signature

1. A spine visible in every section: palette (e.g. orange / electric blue / cream / black), chrome, a dot.
2. Sections with one technique each: easing demo, morph, pattern propagation, 3D point cloud,
   kinetic word, particle formation, character.
3. Transitions that carry a shape from one section into the next (a dot becomes the next scene).
4. Meta content works: animation about animation (easing curves labelled, principles checklist).
5. Ends on the maker's mark with a tagline.
6. Common openers and closers: one dot breathing on an empty ground; the whole frame flipping black ↔
   white between sections; the editor itself on screen (curves, handles, playhead, live readouts);
   a spec card at the end. They are now clichés of the genre: use them knowingly.
7. Gallery variant: a grid of pieces that play as they come into view, each with its prompt. Race
   variant: three versions running side by side with live telemetry (errors shown honestly).

## Camera · rhythm · easing

The strongest reels of Sep 2026 were 15 s with about 8 sections of 1–1.3 s, each on two beats at
120–130 BPM (the length was set by the prompt, so this is what people asked for, not proof that
shorter is better). Reels at 30 s and over, with 1.5–3 s sections, did markedly worse. Every cut on a
downbeat, each section internally fast-slow-fast.

## Sound

One driving track; section changes on bars; sound design matched to each technique.

## Build

Sections as separate groups in one world; the spine (chrome, palette variables) global; each section
a function of local time.

## Breaks when

- No spine (it becomes a random effects compilation); sections of equal length and equal energy.
- Small mono readouts carrying information on screen for one second (unreadable: make them texture
  or give them time).
- Two sections with different colour grading spliced together (the spine breaks).
- It is the 2026 house reel: cream/black, orange-red accent, giant grotesk, `MOTION DESIGNER`,
  `AVAILABLE FOR…`, timecode HUD. Change at least the palette, the type and the copy to the maker's own.
- Results vary wildly: the same one-line prompt produced films from 45 to 17,000 likes. A reel buys
  a high ceiling, not a floor; for a reliable result use the director's treatment first.
