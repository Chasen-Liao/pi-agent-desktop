# card-montage — one line, one card, next

The indie product promo of 2026: a short headline on one side, a rebuilt UI card or phone on the
other; the card springs in and does one thing (a file uploads, a list fills, a check pops), then a
clean cut to the next line. A step indicator or progress bar often ties the screens together; a big
number or a 3D room may punctuate. Twelve of the 54 most-liked prompt-gallery films of Sep–Oct 2026
are this (a Portuguese baby-registry app, a French visa helper, a PDF-to-explainer tool, a CV
builder), and it was the most common shape for a small team's product film.

**Says:** *here is what it does, step by step, and it is finished and real.* Calmer and easier to
read than continuous-morph, more designed than a screen recording.

**Use when:** a consumer or prosumer app with 3–6 clear steps or benefits; a small team with a
landing page to point at; the viewer should be able to retell the steps. **Avoid when:** the product
has one big idea (use kinetic-type or continuous-morph), or the UI is the spectacle (use cursor-demo).

## Signature

1. Split layout: 2–5 words of headline (often with one italic or accent word) beside one UI card or a
   phone. The card is the evidence for the line next to it.
2. Each card does one visible thing while on screen: a field fills, a file lands, a list ticks, a
   ring counts to 100 %. A card that only fades in is a slide.
3. A step system: `STEP 1`, a three-part progress bar, `3/6`. It gives the cuts a reason.
4. Clean hard cuts or quick wipes between screens, on the beat; within a screen everything springs.
5. One completion moment (all steps ticked, the room furnished, `Approved ✓`) before the mark.
6. Ends on the logo, one-line promise and the URL.

## Camera · rhythm · easing

- Mostly a locked frame per screen; a slow 1–2 % drift keeps holds alive. A push into the card for the
  one action that needs reading.
- 1.5–3 s per screen, 110–125 BPM; the headline lands first, the card's action on the next beat.
- Cards `OM.spring(0.08–0.12)`; headline words mask-reveal or blur-to-sharp on the accent word.

## Look

The product's own palette and type, not a house style: the baby app is lavender with an editorial
serif; the PDF tool is off-white with its three book colours. Real UI copy in the user's language.
Soft shadows under cards; device frames only when the platform matters.
**Banned:** cards that appear without doing anything, a different layout on every screen, more than
one sentence per screen, stock icons that don't match the product's own.

## Sound

Light track (lo-fi, soft house, acoustic); a tick or pop on each card action, a whoosh only on the
cuts; one warmer chime on the completion moment. Often fine muted.

## Skeleton (20 s, 120 BPM, 40 beats)

`hook: the problem in one line over a busy field (0–6) → product mark (6–8) → step 1 line + card
action (8–16) → step 2 (16–24) → step 3 (24–30) → completion receipt (30–34) → logo, promise, URL
(34–40)`

## Build

- One scene per step in one world; each scene a function of local time. Keep the layout grid
  identical across scenes so the cut feels like a page turn, not a new film.
- The step indicator is global and updates on each cut.
- Read cursor-demo for the cursor if a card needs a click.

## Breaks when

- Every screen has the same timing and the same entrance (template).
- Headlines say what the card already shows ("Upload files" beside an upload card); the line should
  give the reason, the card the proof.
- The product's colours were replaced by the default cream-and-orange.

## Pairs well with

kinetic-type for the hook line; data-story for a one-number proof; 3d-object for a single "world"
moment (an isometric room filling up).
